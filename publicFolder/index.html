3 Monumental Achievements

Explorer 1,)

Launched in 1958, it became the first U.S. Satelite. Measuring at 80 inches, completed in less than 3 months, orbited the Earth 58,000 times.

Hubble Space telescope)

Launched in 1990, it shifted our view from Ground-Based Telescopes to more capable Space-Telescopes. Although not the first space-telescope, it has made over 1.5 million observations and has 10-20 times the resolution of a ground-based telescope.

Apollo 11)

Launched in 1969, allowed for the first human steps on the Moon, and expanded the frontier for space exploration.

Recent NASA Breakthroughs

Lithium-Fed Thrusters)

Designed for the future of Space Exploration, they are next-gen electric propulsion systems to get humans to Mars.

Electrodynamic Dust Sheilds)

Dust is a huge problem on the Moon, it can damage space suits, technology, and stick to solar panels with its jagged edges. Therefore, by using a electrodynamic currents, it repels most Regolith.

GRX-810 Superalloy)

A revolutionary 3D-printed material that can withstand over 2000 degrees Fahrenheit while lasting longer than traditional aerospace metals.

NASA's Artemis Program

A Multi-Mission campaign aimed at putting Humans back on the Moon. This is comprised of 5 distinct phases. Two of which, have already been meet through Artemis I and Artemis II. These two incredible pioneering missions validated the SLS rocket and Orion Spacecraft with a crew test flight. The 2 upcoming planned missions are set to be completed every year from 2027-2028 will target testing proximity operations, docking-procedures, Human Landing Systems, and Axiom Space Suits. The final Artemis V mission focuses on repeated surface landings, deploying pressurized and unpressurized rovers, and building starting elements of a lunar base camp.
